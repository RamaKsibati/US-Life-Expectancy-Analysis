# Analysis of Life Expectancy in the United States

**Statistical Research · Data Visualization · Academic Team Project**

**Tools:** Minitab · Tableau · OpenMarkov · Microsoft Excel  
**Academic recognition:** **Nominated to participate in Senior Design Day**, as reported by the project team member.

This four-person university research project explored how life expectancy differs across U.S. states and how it is associated with socioeconomic, demographic, environmental and public-health indicators. It used statistical software rather than custom programming.

> **About the evidence:** The original group presentation documents the historical dataset and the team's analyses; it should not be interpreted as a current public-health study or causal proof. A number of conclusions in the original slides are exploratory and were not independently revalidated for this portfolio summary.

## Project overview

The team compared state-level life expectancy with indicators such as:

- Education and poverty
- Homicide rates and crime index
- Alcohol consumption and unemployment
- Flu vaccination and COVID-19 indicators
- Air quality, snowfall and temperature
- Household and living-wage factors

## What we did

1. Combined and examined state-level data from multiple sources.
2. Reviewed missing records, excluded non-state entries where appropriate, and identified potential outliers using boxplots.
3. Compared variables with descriptive statistics, fitted distributions, correlations and regression.
4. Conducted hypothesis tests and confidence-interval analyses.
5. Used Bayesian network and predictive-modeling tools to investigate conditional relationships.
6. Presented graphs, findings, limitations and individual research sections.

### A representative finding

A fitted-line analysis of alcohol consumption and life expectancy showed no strong linear association, despite exploratory patterns that initially appeared positive. This reinforced the need to distinguish a plotted pattern from strong evidence of a relationship.

## Ethical presentation of results

The historical data represent state-level associations, not evidence that an individual behavioral factor caused a change in longevity. Outlier replacement/deletion decisions can influence results. Some slides use causal wording or inconsistent labels; the case study describes the analyses as exploratory and does not silently alter the original slides.
